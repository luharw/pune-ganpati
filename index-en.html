1. Shri Kasba Ganpati

Gramdaivat of Pune • Established 1630 CE
Historical Insight: Consecrated by Rajmata Jijabai and young Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj when Pune was rebuilt after being destroyed by Bijapur forces. An ancient, self-manifested (swayambhu) stone idol was found near Vinayak Thakar's home. Shivaji Maharaj plowed the nearby soil with a ceremonial golden plow and prayed here before embarking on his campaigns for Swarajya.
Visarjan Protocol: Holds the supreme 1st place of honor and officially leads Pune's immersion procession on Anant Chaturdashi in a silver palanquin (Chaandi cha Rath). City police hold back all general mandals until Kasba Ganpati reaches the immersion ghat.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Strict No-Gulal Rule: No orange gulal powder is thrown onto this idol or its palanquin to prevent chemical erosion of the antique silver. The farewell aarti before visarjan is traditionally conducted exclusively by women devotees.
Visual Cue: Illuminated top signboard reading 'मानाचा पहिला ॥ श्री कसबा गणपती ॥' with twin sculpted silver lions flanking the silver throne.

2. Shri Tambdi Jogeshwari Ganpati

Goddess Jogeshwari Shrine Deity • Established 1893 CE
Historical Insight: Dedicated to Goddess Jogeshwari, the patron goddess (Gramdevi) of Pune. The temple dates back to the 15th century, and the public festival was started in its courtyard in 1893. The Peshwas sought blessings here before undertaking military expeditions.
Visarjan Protocol: Marches 2nd along Laxmi Road on the legendary 'Sheshrath'—a grand chariot crested with the multi-headed serpent Sheshnaag.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Generational Clay Sculpting: Hand-sculpted every year from natural river clay (shadu maati) by the Dhumal artisan family in an unchanging posture: seated in Abhayamudra while holding tridents (trishuls). Like Kasba Ganpati, it strictly prohibits gulal spraying.
Visual Cue: Idol seated inside a dark carved antique silver temple arch (makhar), holding tridents and draped in yellow and purple silk pitambar.

3. Shri Guruji Talim Ganpati

Symbol of Harmony & Valour • Established 1887 CE
Historical Insight: Founded six years before Tilak popularized public Ganeshotsav by wrestling masters (ustads) Bhiku Shinde and Nana Khasgiwale alongside their Muslim wrestling peers from a traditional gymnasium (talim).
Visarjan Protocol: Marches 3rd in the Laxmi Road procession, acclaimed for massive flower-decked display floats (Pushparath).
Lesser-Known Trivia: Pioneer of Interfaith Brotherhood: Established and financed jointly by Hindu and Muslim wrestlers, with Muslim families historically managing festival logistics. It was also the first mandal in Pune to introduce grand artistic decorative floats.
Visual Cue: Radiant, fully golden sculpted sanctum wall with a circular halo; pedestal explicitly inscribed with 'गुरुजी तालीमचा गणपती'.

4. Shri Tulshibaug Ganpati

The Grand Clay Idol of Pune • Established 1901 CE
Historical Insight: Established by merchants, brass-smiths, and traders of Pune's busiest retail market (Tulshibaug) in 1901. Pioneered the tradition of giant, life-sized clay murtis in the city.
Visarjan Protocol: 4th in the procession hierarchy. Carried on a heavy-duty mechanized chariot due to the immense weight of the idol and its jewelry.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Over 80 kg of Pure Silver: The towering ~14-foot clay idol is dressed in over 80 kg of genuine silver ornaments, armor, and weapons (axe and trident). The Khatavkar family has sculpted this massive murti annually for over a century using specialized bamboo/straw armatures.
Visual Cue: Majestic ~14-foot clay murti holding an axe and trident, wearing an ornate tiered silver crown with devotees snapping photos below.

5. Shri Kesariwada Ganpati

Cradle of India's Freedom Awakening • Established 1894 CE
Historical Insight: Started by Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak at Vinchurkar Wada and shifted in 1905 to Kesariwada, headquarters of his anti-colonial newspapers 'Kesari' and 'Mahratta'. Tilak used this festival platform to bypass British anti-assembly laws and mobilize citizens for Swarajya.
Visarjan Protocol: Completes and closes the prestigious 5 Manache Ganpatis. General city mandals are only permitted to enter the main procession after Kesariwada passes.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Authentic Wooden Palkhi: Shuns trucks and tractors; the modest eco-friendly clay idol is carried on the shoulders of volunteers in an antique wooden palanquin. Freedom icons like Swami Vivekananda, Mahatma Gandhi, and Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose delivered speeches here.
Visual Cue: Side profile of traditional clay murti seated on a royal blue velvet chowrang, with fresh coconuts and fruit offerings arranged in front.

Shrimant Dagdusheth Halwai Ganpati

World-Renowned Divine Splendour • Established 1893 CE
Historical Insight: Founded by Lingayat sweetmaker Dagdusheth Gadve and his wife Lakshmibai after losing their only son to the plague. On their spiritual guru's advice, they channeled their grief and wealth into establishing this shrine. Tilak was a close associate and attended the installation.
Visarjan Protocol: Supported by one of India's richest charitable trusts funding hospitals, schools, and disaster relief. Famous for commissioning monumental temporary replicas of ancient Indian temple architecture.
Lesser-Known Trivia: 40+ kg of Solid Gold: The idol is adorned with over 40 kilograms of pure gold, studded with diamonds and gems. On Rishi Panchami, 30,000+ women gather at dawn in traditional attire to chant the Atharvashirsha in unison.
Visual Cue: World-famous gold-covered idol seated beneath a dark carved stone archway decorated with sculpted peacock motifs.

Akhil Mandai Mandal (Sharada Gajanan)

Goddess Sharada & Ganesha • Established 1894 CE
Historical Insight: Founded by the vegetable and fruit vendors of Pune's historic municipal market (Mahatma Phule Mandai), honoring the city's working-class heritage.
Visarjan Protocol: Visarjan takes place on a grand ceremonial swing (Jhula) covered in flowers that gently sways throughout the procession.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Rare Dual Murti: The only prominent Ganesha mandal in India featuring Lord Ganesha seated alongside Goddess Sharada (Saraswati), the goddess of wisdom and arts, symbolizing the fusion of intellect and learning.
Visual Cue: Wide landscape orientation showing Lord Ganesha seated side-by-side with Goddess Sharada draped in a bright yellow Paithani saree.

Shrimant Bhausaheb Rangari Ganpati

India's First Public Ganeshotsav Idol • Established 1892 CE
Historical Insight: Installed in 1892 by Ayurvedic physician and undercover revolutionary Bhausaheb Laxman Javale (Rangari)—one year before Tilak publicized the festival. Rangari used religious gatherings as cover to organize underground armed resistance against British rule.
Visarjan Protocol: The unique warrior-pose idol portrays Ganesha subduing a winged demon (asura) with a trishul, serving as an allegorical call for Indians to overthrow colonial oppression.
Lesser-Known Trivia: 134-Year-Old Original Murti: The original 1892 idol—crafted from paper-pulp, wood, and vegetable resins—is never immersed and has been worshipped continuously for over 130 years. Rangari's historic wada contained secret chambers for storing revolutionary weapons.
Visual Cue: Warrior-pose Ganesha slaying a blue demon with a trident, inside a pink scalloped archway with the Asia Book of Records certificate.

Punyacha Raja (Sadashiv Peth)

Iconic Mandal of Sadashiv Peth
Historical Insight: A cornerstone devotional mandal of Sadashiv Peth—a neighborhood long revered as the heart of traditional Marathi scholarship, performing arts, and heritage.
Visarjan Protocol: Renowned for elaborate themed stage settings replicating Maratha hill forts (Raigad, Sinhagad) and royal Peshwa darbars.
Lesser-Known Trivia: Heroic Equestrian Stance: Rather than standard seated postures, Punyacha Raja is famously sculpted in a warrior pose seated atop his celestial vehicle, the mouse (Mushak).
Visual Cue: Golden Ganesha in an active warrior pose mounted on his mouse vehicle beneath an illuminated golden 'पुण्याचा राजा' sign.
